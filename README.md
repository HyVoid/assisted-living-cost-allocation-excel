### Workbook Architecture

The workbook is organized as a one-directional analytical chain:

```text
00_Setup
   │
   ├── System parameters
   ├── Unit master data
   ├── Account master data
   └── Shared-cost allocation rules
          │
          ▼
01_Transactions
   │
   ├── Direct transactions
   └── Shared transactions
          │
          ▼
02_Allocation_Engine
   │
   ├── Direct → 1-to-1 unit assignment
   └── Shared → 1-to-many rule-based expansion
          │
          ├──────────────────────────────┐
          ▼                              ▼
03_Unit_PL                     04_Consolidated_PL
   │                              │
   ├── Unit revenue               ├── Consolidated P&L
   ├── Direct cost                ├── Source total
   ├── Contribution margin        ├── Allocated total
   ├── Allocated shared cost      ├── Unit roll-up
   ├── Total cost                 └── Reconciliation
   └── Net income
          │
          └───────────────┐
                          ▼
                   05_Dashboard
```

| Sheet                  | Role                        | Primary Responsibility                                       |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `00_Setup`             | Configuration & master data | Parameters, units, accounts, allocation rules                |
| `01_Transactions`      | Source data                 | Single entry point for original transactions                 |
| `02_Allocation_Engine` | Calculation engine          | Converts source transactions into unit-level analytical rows |
| `03_Unit_PL`           | Unit profitability          | Monthly or annual profitability by operating unit            |
| `04_Consolidated_PL`   | Consolidation & audit       | Company P&L and three-way reconciliation                     |
| `05_Dashboard`         | Management view             | KPIs, unit ranking, shared-cost burden, trends               |

The architecture deliberately separates **source data from allocation logic**.

A transaction does not become a different transaction simply because it is allocated to several units. The original transaction remains the source record. The allocation engine creates analytical rows derived from it.

This provides an audit path:

```text
Source Transaction
      ↓
Allocation Rule
      ↓
Target Unit + Applied Rate
      ↓
Allocated Amount
      ↓
Unit P&L
      ↓
Consolidated P&L
      ↓
Reconciliation
```

### Three Traps That Catch Even Experienced Management Accountants

#### Trap 1 — Entering Shared Costs as Separate Unit Transactions

**1. Decision made**

Management wants to show the $3,000 vehicle expense across three facilities.

**2. Faulty number**

Instead of retaining one $3,000 source transaction, the operator enters three separate expenses.

**3. Recommendation changes**

The unit reports may appear correct, but the source ledger no longer has a clean one-to-one relationship with the management report.

**4. Why the reasoning is incorrect**

The allocation is being performed during data entry rather than as a controlled analytical step. That makes it difficult to distinguish the original economic event from the reporting treatment.

**5. Corrected approach**

Record:

```text
Vehicle Expense
Source Amount = $3,000
Rule = VEH-001
```

Then apply:

```text
Unit A = 40% → $1,200
Unit B = 35% → $1,050
Unit C = 25% → $750
```

**6. Corrected outcome**

The three unit reports receive the correct amounts while the original $3,000 transaction remains intact and traceable.

**7. Formula**

<details>
<summary>Allocation amount</summary>

```excel
=ROUND(Tx_Amount * Applied_Rate, 2)
```

The source amount is multiplied by the rule percentage only after the transaction has passed the input validation layer.

</details>

---

#### Trap 2 — Treating a 95% Allocation as “Close Enough”

**1. Decision made**

A corporate overhead rule distributes costs across four facilities.

**2. Faulty assumption**

The entered percentages total 95%.

**3. Recommendation changes**

The resulting unit P&L understates the amount absorbed by the operating units. The missing 5% effectively disappears from the allocation view.

**4. Why the reasoning is incorrect**

A shared expense is still a full economic event. A rule that distributes only 95% has not completely represented that event.

**5. Corrected approach**

The rule must explicitly reconcile to 100%, subject only to the configured numerical tolerance.

**6. Corrected outcome**

A rule such as:

```text
Unit A   25%
Unit B   25%
Unit C   25%
Unit D   25%
Total   100%
```

is accepted. A 95% or 105% rule is flagged before it can drive normal downstream allocation.

**7. Formula**

<details>
<summary>100% allocation validation</summary>

```excel
=LET(
    total_pct, [@[Rule_Total_Check]],
    tolerance, cfg_Tolerance,
    diff, total_pct - 1,
    IF(
        ABS(diff) <= tolerance,
        "✅ OK",
        IF(
            diff < 0,
            "⚠️ Under 100%",
            "❌ Over 100%"
        )
    )
)
```

The tolerance is centrally controlled rather than hard-coded into the rule.

</details>

---

#### Trap 3 — Trusting Unit Profitability Without Reconciling the Company Total

**1. Decision made**

A regional manager reviews the unit P&Ls and sees that each facility has a plausible expense total.

**2. Faulty number**

The individual reports look reasonable, but the combined allocated amount does not exactly match the source expense population.

**3. Recommendation changes**

Management may conclude that the facilities collectively generated a certain margin when the underlying allocation process has actually omitted or duplicated cost.

**4. Why the reasoning is incorrect**

Plausible individual numbers do not prove that the reporting system is complete.

**5. Corrected approach**

The company total must be checked through three linked populations:

```text
Source Transactions
        ≡
Allocation Engine
        ≡
Unit P&L Roll-up
```

**6. Corrected outcome**

A zero reconciliation difference provides evidence that the allocation process has neither silently dropped nor created expense.

**7. Formula**

<details>
<summary>Source-to-allocation reconciliation</summary>

```excel
=IF(
    ABS(
        CPL_Source_Total_Expense
        - CPL_Allocated_Total_Expense
    ) <= cfg_Tolerance,
    "✅ Reconciled",
    "❌ Difference Requires Review"
)
```

The same control can be extended to compare the allocation-engine total against the aggregated unit-level expense.

</details>

### Example Scenario

Consider an assisted living company operating three active units:

| Unit      |      Revenue | Direct Expense |
| --------- | -----------: | -------------: |
| Unit A    |     $180,000 |       $126,000 |
| Unit B    |     $150,000 |       $112,500 |
| Unit C    |     $120,000 |        $96,000 |
| **Total** | **$450,000** |   **$334,500** |

Before shared costs, the combined contribution is:

```text
$450,000 − $334,500 = $115,500
```

The company also incurs two shared expenses during the month:

| Shared Expense     |  Amount | Allocation      |
| ------------------ | ------: | --------------- |
| Vehicle expenses   |  $3,000 | 40% / 35% / 25% |
| Corporate overhead | $24,000 | 25% / 35% / 40% |

The resulting shared-cost burden is:

| Unit      |    Vehicle |   Corporate | Total Shared Cost |
| --------- | ---------: | ----------: | ----------------: |
| Unit A    |     $1,200 |      $6,000 |            $7,200 |
| Unit B    |     $1,050 |      $8,400 |            $9,450 |
| Unit C    |       $750 |      $9,600 |           $10,350 |
| **Total** | **$3,000** | **$24,000** |       **$27,000** |

Unit profitability then becomes:

| Unit   | Contribution | Shared Cost | Net Income | Net Margin |
| ------ | -----------: | ----------: | ---------: | ---------: |
| Unit A |      $54,000 |      $7,200 |    $46,800 |      26.0% |
| Unit B |      $37,500 |      $9,450 |    $28,050 |      18.7% |
| Unit C |      $24,000 |     $10,350 |    $13,650 |      11.4% |

The important management conclusion is not simply that Unit C has the lowest margin.

The model makes the reasoning more precise:

* Unit C already has the weakest contribution before shared costs.
* It also absorbs the largest corporate-overhead percentage.
* Its final margin therefore falls to 11.4%.
* Unit A remains comfortably above the configured target margin.
* Unit C becomes the clearest candidate for operational review.

At company level:

```text
Revenue
= $450,000

Direct Expense
= $334,500

Shared Expense
= $27,000

Consolidated Net Income
= $88,500
```

The $27,000 of shared costs must reconcile exactly to the $3,000 vehicle transaction plus the $24,000 corporate-overhead transaction.

The allocation does not change the company's total expense. It changes **where management sees the expense**.


### Formula Reference

The workbook relies on a small set of modern Excel functions. The design goal is not formula complexity for its own sake. Each formula exists to enforce one business rule: **classify correctly, allocate completely, aggregate consistently, and reconcile the result**.

<details>
<summary>00_Setup — Allocation Rule Controls</summary>

#### Rule total by allocation ID

```excel
=SUMIFS(
    tbl_Allocation_Rules[Rule_Percent],
    tbl_Allocation_Rules[Rule_ID],
    [@Rule_ID]
)
```

**Purpose:** Calculates the complete allocation percentage for each `Rule_ID`.

Because one rule may contain multiple target units, `SUMIFS` aggregates every row belonging to the same rule.

A valid rule must total 100%, subject to `cfg_Tolerance`.

#### Rule status

```excel
=LET(
    total_pct, [@[Rule_Total_Check]],
    tolerance, cfg_Tolerance,
    diff, total_pct - 1,
    IF(
        ABS(diff) <= tolerance,
        "✅ OK",
        IF(
            diff < 0,
            "⚠️ 欠分配",
            "❌ 超额分配"
        )
    )
)
```

**Purpose:** Prevent an incomplete or excessive allocation rule from being treated as valid.

The tolerance is centralized so that the same numerical control can be reused throughout the workbook.

#### Unique rule audit list

```excel
=LET(
    u_rules, UNIQUE(tbl_Allocation_Rules[Rule_ID]),
    rule_desc,
        XLOOKUP(
            u_rules,
            tbl_Allocation_Rules[Rule_ID],
            tbl_Allocation_Rules[Rule_Desc]
        ),
    rule_sum,
        MAP(
            u_rules,
            LAMBDA(
                r,
                SUMIFS(
                    tbl_Allocation_Rules[Rule_Percent],
                    tbl_Allocation_Rules[Rule_ID],
                    r
                )
            )
        ),
    rule_chk,
        IF(
            ABS(rule_sum - 1) <= cfg_Tolerance,
            "✅ 闭环",
            "❌ 异常需核查"
        ),
    HSTACK(
        u_rules,
        rule_desc,
        rule_sum,
        rule_chk
    )
)
```

**Purpose:** Produces one management-level audit row per allocation rule rather than requiring users to inspect individual rule records manually.

`UNIQUE` identifies the rules, `MAP` calculates each rule's allocation total, and `HSTACK` assembles the audit view.

</details>

<summary>02_Allocation_Engine — Direct and Shared Allocation</summary>

#### Direct transaction treatment

A valid direct transaction is effectively a 1-to-1 mapping:

```text
Source Transaction
       ↓
One Unit
       ↓
100% Applied Rate
       ↓
Original Amount
```

The applied rate is therefore:

```excel
=1
```

The allocated amount remains the original transaction amount.

#### Shared transaction treatment

Shared transactions require a 1-to-many transformation.

For example:

```text
TX-2026-0001
$3,000
VEH-001
     │
     ├── UNIT-A   40%   $1,200
     ├── UNIT-B   35%   $1,050
     └── UNIT-C   25%   $750
```

The workbook uses `FILTER`, `REDUCE`, `LAMBDA`, `HSTACK`, `VSTACK`, and `EXPAND` to construct this expanded analytical population.

The core amount calculation is:

```excel
=ROUND(
    row_amt * target_pcts,
    2
)
```

**Purpose:** Converts a single shared economic event into multiple unit-level analytical rows without modifying the original source transaction.

#### Full allocation-engine pattern

```excel
=LET(
    valid_tx,
        FILTER(
            tbl_Transactions,
            tbl_Transactions[Tx_Calc_Audit_Alert] = "✅ 校验通过"
        ),

    is_direct,
        CHOOSECOLS(valid_tx, 5) = "Direct",

    is_shared,
        CHOOSECOLS(valid_tx, 5) = "Shared",

    /* Direct rows are passed through at 100%. */

    /* Shared rows are matched against
       tbl_Allocation_Rules and expanded
       into target-unit rows. */

    /* Final output is assembled with VSTACK
       and receives a sequential allocation-row ID. */
)
```

The important architectural point is that the engine does **not** ask the user to calculate the allocation manually.

It receives:

```text
Transaction + Rule ID
```

and produces:

```text
Transaction + Target Unit + Applied Rate + Allocated Amount
```

The engine therefore becomes the controlled boundary between source accounting data and management reporting.

</details>

<summary>04_Consolidated_PL — Three-Way Reconciliation</summary>

The consolidation layer intentionally separates reporting from reconciliation.

Three populations are compared:

```text
Source Transactions
        ≡
Allocation Engine
        ≡
Unit P&L Roll-up
```

#### Source expense total

```excel
=LET(
    target_month, sel_Consol_Month,
    valid_tx,
        FILTER(
            tbl_Transactions,
            tbl_Transactions[Tx_Calc_Audit_Alert] = "✅ 校验通过"
        ),
    tx_cat, CHOOSECOLS(valid_tx, 11),
    tx_month, CHOOSECOLS(valid_tx, 9),
    tx_amt, CHOOSECOLS(valid_tx, 6),

    IF(
        target_month = "All-Year",
        SUMIFS(tx_amt, tx_cat, "<>Revenue"),
        SUMIFS(
            tx_amt,
            tx_cat, "<>Revenue",
            tx_month, target_month
        )
    )
)
```

#### Allocation-engine expense total

```excel
=LET(
    target_month, sel_Consol_Month,
    eng_months,
        INDEX('02_Allocation_Engine'!A3#, , 3),
    eng_accts,
        INDEX('02_Allocation_Engine'!A3#, , 5),
    eng_amts,
        INDEX('02_Allocation_Engine'!A3#, , 9),

    is_exp,
        LEFT(eng_accts, 3) = "EXP",

    IF(
        target_month = "All-Year",
        SUM(FILTER(eng_amts, is_exp, 0)),
        SUM(
            FILTER(
                eng_amts,
                is_exp * (eng_months = target_month),
                0
            )
        )
    )
)
```

### Validation Rules

The validation framework operates at several levels. Input validation protects the source population; rule validation protects allocation logic; reconciliation protects the final reporting population.

| Field / Control          | Rule                                                             | Error Behavior                                             |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| `Tx_ID`                  | Must identify a unique source transaction                        | Duplicate records should be investigated before reporting  |
| `Tx_Date`                | Must contain a valid transaction date                            | Invalid date prevents reliable monthly classification      |
| Analysis year            | Transaction year should match `cfg_AnalysisYear`                 | `⚠️` cross-year warning                                    |
| `Tx_Amount`              | Must be greater than zero                                        | `❌` transaction rejected from valid calculation population |
| `Tx_Account_Code`        | Must exist in account master                                     | `❌` unknown account                                        |
| `Tx_Cost_Type`           | Must be `Direct` or `Shared`                                     | Invalid classification must be corrected                   |
| Direct transaction       | Must specify one `Tx_Direct_Unit_ID`                             | `❌` missing unit                                           |
| Direct transaction       | Must not specify an allocation rule                              | `❌` conflicting assignment                                 |
| Shared transaction       | Must specify one valid `Tx_Allocation_Rule_ID`                   | `❌` missing rule                                           |
| Shared transaction       | Must not specify a direct unit                                   | `❌` conflicting assignment                                 |
| Shared allocation rule   | Total percentage must equal 100% within tolerance                | `⚠️` under-allocation or `❌` over-allocation               |
| Revenue classification   | Revenue cannot be treated as a shared expense                    | `❌` classification conflict                                |
| Active unit              | Unit must exist in the unit master                               | Transaction should not flow to an undefined reporting unit |
| Allocation result        | Allocated amount must derive from source amount and applied rate | Any unexplained difference requires review                 |
| Source vs Engine         | Source expense must reconcile to engine expense                  | Reconciliation failure                                     |
| Engine vs Unit P&L       | Engine expense must reconcile to unit roll-up                    | Reconciliation failure                                     |
| Three-way reconciliation | All reporting populations must remain within `cfg_Tolerance`     | Consolidated audit status turns red                        |
| Dynamic-array spill area | Must remain unobstructed                                         | Excel displays `#SPILL!`                                   |
| Formula cells            | Must not be overwritten manually                                 | Calculation chain becomes invalid                          |
| Reporting month          | Must be `All-Year` or a valid available period                   | Invalid selection should not be used for reporting         |

### Operating Boundaries and Maintenance

The workbook is designed around modern Excel rather than legacy spreadsheet compatibility.

**Supported environment**

* Microsoft 365 on Windows or Mac.
* Office 2021 or later.
* Dynamic-array functions including `LET`, `FILTER`, `UNIQUE`, `SORT`, `HSTACK`, `VSTACK`, `MAP`, `LAMBDA`, `REDUCE`, and `EXPAND`.

Older Excel versions may return `_xlfn` errors or fail to evaluate the dynamic-array calculation chain.

**Recommended data volume**

The model is designed for approximately **100,000 source transaction rows** as a practical operating range.

For organizations with substantially larger annual transaction populations, the source architecture recommends separating annual workbooks rather than forcing a single workbook to become an enterprise-scale database.

**Multi-user operation**

The workbook can be stored through OneDrive or SharePoint for collaborative access. However, rule maintenance and month-end reconciliation are control-sensitive activities.

The preferred operating practice is:

```text
Routine transaction entry
        ↓
Collaborative access where appropriate
        ↓
Controlled rule maintenance
        ↓
Dedicated month-end review
        ↓
Reconciliation sign-off
```

The allocation-rule table should not be simultaneously edited by multiple users during a close process.

### Standard Monthly Operating Procedure

The intended recurring workflow is:

```text
1. Maintain allocation rules
          ↓
2. Enter / import transactions
          ↓
3. Review transaction validation
          ↓
4. Confirm consolidated reconciliation
          ↓
5. Review unit profitability
          ↓
6. Present dashboard results
```

#### Step 1 — Review allocation rules

Confirm:

* analysis year;
* active units;
* account master;
* shared-cost allocation percentages.

Every active allocation rule should show a valid 100% status.

#### Step 2 — Load transactions

Direct transactions identify their unit.

Shared transactions identify their allocation rule.

A shared expense remains a single source transaction:

```text
Correct:
$10,000 Corporate Overhead
Rule = CORP-001

Incorrect:
$2,500 Unit A
$2,500 Unit B
$2,500 Unit C
$2,500 Unit D
```

The second approach destroys the distinction between the source event and its management allocation.

#### Step 3 — Run transaction validation

Filter the audit-alert column and resolve all errors before relying on downstream profitability.

A transaction that fails validation should not be treated as equivalent to a valid transaction simply because its amount appears reasonable.

#### Step 4 — Confirm reconciliation

The consolidated audit center should show the green balanced state before management reporting.

If it does not, the dashboard should not be treated as final.

#### Step 5 — Review unit profitability

Switch between `All-Year` and individual months.

Review:

* revenue;
* direct cost;
* contribution margin;
* allocated shared cost;
* total cost;
* net income;
* profit margin;
* health classification.

The key question is whether weak profitability comes from local operating economics or shared-cost absorption.

#### Step 6 — Present management results

The dashboard condenses the validated unit-level population into:

* company revenue;
* company expense;
* company net income;
* company margin;
* top-performing unit;
* lowest-performing unit;
* shared-cost burden.

The dashboard is therefore the final presentation layer, not the source of truth.

### Troubleshooting

#### `#SPILL!` appears in the workbook

Dynamic-array formulas require their intended spill area to remain clear.

Typical causes include:

* manually entered values;
* hidden spaces;
* copied formatting combined with content;
* merged cells;
* another formula occupying the spill range.

Select the source formula cell and inspect the highlighted spill boundary. Remove the obstructing content from the spill range rather than copying the formula downward manually.

#### A new unit does not appear in the P&L

Check:

1. The unit exists in `tbl_Dim_Unit`.
2. `Dim_Unit_Status` is `Active`.
3. The unit ID is unique.
4. No content blocks the dynamic-array output.

The reporting matrix is driven by active-unit master data rather than a fixed list of columns.

#### A shared expense does not allocate

Check:

1. `Tx_Cost_Type = Shared`.
2. `Tx_Allocation_Rule_ID` is populated.
3. The rule exists.
4. The rule status is `OK`.
5. The target units exist.
6. The percentages total 100% within the configured tolerance.

#### Consolidated reconciliation is red

Do not manually adjust the reconciliation output.

Trace the chain:

```text
Source Transaction
       ↓
Transaction Validation
       ↓
Allocation Rule
       ↓
Allocation Engine
       ↓
Unit P&L
       ↓
Consolidated Roll-up
```

The purpose of the reconciliation layer is precisely to identify where the chain stopped agreeing.

## The Business Logic & Methodology

The model uses a simple management-accounting principle: **the source transaction remains intact, while the reporting treatment is applied separately**. This makes shared-cost allocation visible without confusing allocation with the original economic event.

* **Single-source transaction control** — Shared costs are recorded once and then distributed according to an explicit business rule. This reduces duplicate entry and preserves a clean source trail. The management decision becomes: *which units should absorb this cost, and according to what agreed basis?*

* **Rule-based cost allocation** — Each shared expense points to a defined distribution rule. Because the rule must close to 100%, management can distinguish a genuine allocation from an incomplete estimate. The decision becomes whether the allocation basis is commercially appropriate, rather than whether the spreadsheet happened to add up.

* **Contribution profitability analysis** — Direct operating economics are separated from shared-cost absorption. This prevents a unit with strong local operations from being judged solely on a fully loaded result without understanding what is driving the difference.

* **Threshold-based operating alerts** — Profitability is compared with a configurable management target. This turns a long financial report into a prioritized operating review: which units are performing well, which need cost control, and which require immediate attention.

* **Three-way reconciliation** — Source expenses, allocated expenses, and unit-level expenses are compared before management reporting. This ensures that the allocation process changes the *location of cost*, not the company's total cost.

The methodology is therefore less about producing another P&L and more about making the chain from **economic event → allocation → unit economics → consolidated result** auditable and understandable.
